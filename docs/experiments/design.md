# Experiment design guide

For me. Opened when designing an experiment or reading its results. Mirrors the template's sections. Each entry: the problem, examples of it, the advice, the example solution.

## General

1. **The 90/10 law:** always ask, what is the 10% of the work that gets 90% of the learning? Applies to everything below: assumptions, measures, build.

## Learn

### Leap-of-faith assumptions

#### Stripping the explosion down

If the brain explodes into a billion assumptions, u start with 1 and end up with this actual dump from exp1:

1. Reporters can't cover their entire beat throughout the day as fast as AI can.
2. Reporters/aggregators value a tool synthesizing a story across multiple sources saying the same thing.
3. They have monitoring sources I can scrape, like X and websites.
4. They will trust an AI tool to monitor their sources and report accurately in real time, deduped and synthesized.
   1. Trusting its synthesis misses nothing versus their own reading of the source.
   2. Trusting its latency is as fast as their own alerts.
   3. Trusting its filtration doesn't skip what's needed.
5. The combined tool provides enough value to pay for after a 7-day trial.
6. Setting up custom desks beforehand and sending them over will be enough to pull their attention to Oparax.
7. They want to be notified on their phone, so the X DM surface removes the friction of sitting on the site.
   1. DMing Oparax's account is less friction than signing up.

**Solution:**

- Dump freely first then keep only the beliefs this one run can answer, written at the level the run can decide. Wording like "as currently built" absorbs the whole substack (which model, speed, accuracy) so it stops demanding answers.
- The deeper worries need no answers now they are moreso "company" assumptions. If the run fails, the failure itself points at which one to open.

How the dump cleaned up: 3 became who to pick (a filter you apply, not test). 6 and 7 became how the run works (mechanics you use, not test). 4's trust worries plus the model/cost/speed stack got absorbed by "as currently built" and parked as company assumptions. 2 got parked untested, nobody ever asked for it. 1 and 5 survived as the bet.

#### Splitting an assumption

What survived was first written as one line: monitoring alone is worth paying for, they'll use it unprompted and at least 1 will pay. Is that 1 assumption or 2?

**Keep two assumptions separate only when they fail in different directions.** If they would fail the same way, they are one assumption.

The final 2:

1. Monitoring alone is valuable: aggregators of any type of news on X using public sources will use Oparax's monitoring without being prompted.
2. Monitoring alone is a payable product: at least 1 of them will pay to keep it after a trial.

Separate because "nobody used it" kills the idea, while "used it, nobody paid" means the value is real and the money side needs work.
