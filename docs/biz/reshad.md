# Reshad Rahman, the one real user

Football transfer reporter (FC Barcelona and the wider transfer market), based in Dubai, large following, recognized by name at a San Francisco hackathon. He is the founder's cousin, and that governs how every line below is read: his praise is discounted (family flatters, and he answered messages out of family obligation as much as interest), his criticisms are upgraded (family volunteering negatives unprompted is unusually honest), and his responsiveness proves nothing about strangers. They also talk outside X (BOTIM) in a mix of English, Hindi and Urdu.

## Epoch 1: Mid July, workflow and economics (before ever seeing the product)

Answers to a structured question set on 2026-07-19, all verbatim:

- **On anticipating a break:** "I actually knew before hand that he would speak up after a match, which he did, so I was just waiting."
- **On speed:** "I posted it as soon as it came out, not even 10 mins late, right there. This is how I post news all the time, the second time it's out it goes to my profile."
- **On sources:** "I mainly just read articles from Spanish and English websites, like Mundodeportivo or Athletic UK." And the proportion: "95% of the news comes from articles actually."
- **On his first 60 seconds:** "The second it comes out, I first read it myself. Then, I understand the news. Once I know what the news is, I begin typing it out as a tweet, including the source in the end. Once im done writing the tweet, I post it. I try my best to not copy/paste exactly, and always try to make sure I report it in my own words."
- **On navigating news sites:** "I simply just go to the club, so for instance if I wanna catch up on everything 'Barcelona related', I'll click the Barça logo or the Barça tag."
- **On platforms:** "On X, it's more simple, I can share 5-10 reports within 30 minutes and it's still fine. While on Instagram, you cannot exactly do that. Instead, I only post just the major update and breaking news."
- **His economics, the willingness-to-pay ceiling for this segment:** roughly $400 to $800 a month from X and about $1,000 from Instagram. On what drives X revenue: "It actually depends mainly on how many 'verified' users are interacting with your posts (this includes comments, reposts, quoted tweets, etc). One thing to note: X revenue doesn't depend on how many likes in total a post has."
- **Network reality:** asked for introductions, he explained Fabrizio Romano charges significant fees for promotion and does not do promo over messages, but supplied his email. He confirmed he knows Sacha Tavolieri.

**Learning:** his craft is speed plus rewriting in his own words; his raw material is overwhelmingly articles, not X posts; his income caps what any tool in this segment could charge.

## Epoch 4: August 10 to 11, the accuracy failure and the reframe

- **The Deco error, self-reported without being asked:** "As I want to be fully honest: A slight mistranslation/nuances error. Deco (Barcelona's director) never actually confirmed it, unlike what the article actually says." A Spanish article said Deco "opens the door" to signing a defender; the draft said Deco "has confirmed" it.
- **Why it matters, his explanation:** "Deco is Barcelona's director, the one who is in charge of signing new players in the team. If he publicly says something about a new signing, it's of course as factual as it can get. Any fan would prefer hearing the words of someone in the club over a report of a news article." And: "the context is actually that a journalist (who actually isn't even too reliable) mentions that according to his information Deco is simply open to the idea." His conclusion: "I wouldn't post that because people would think that it's Deco himself saying it yk."
- **Interpretation:** this is a class of error, not a one-off bug: promoting a secondhand, low-confidence report into a direct statement by a principal. For a reporter whose standing rests on accuracy, one such post is reputationally costly. This is the product's accuracy bar.
- **Speed restated, sharper:** "if I read something worth posting, I'll post it right away. If a news comes out, I post there and then. But on Operax, I may have to wait for even 1-2 minutes. As mentioned, speed is the among the most important things for a reporter in general."
- **After a freshness fix (Aug 11):** "Tested it a little, and yes it does seem to be more fresh now. There are some areas which would need minor tweaks, like the hashtags. under topics which aren't transfer related, it is still recognizing them as #Transfers. But other than that, it's really really well put."
- **The line that redefines the product:** "This is something a reporter can very well use after waking up or to catch up on stuff too."

## Epoch 5: August 12, the verdict

Asked directly why he was not posting from Oparax:

- **The most important customer evidence on record:** "The thing is, I've been using the agent to catch up on stuff and mainly see if I've not missed out on anything. Whenever I post something, it's usually very extensive. Most of my news are very long. Like I said yesterday, it's very very helpful to me to catch up on everything because it makes things so much easier. I hope that's okay."
- **And immediately after:** "I'll be more than happy to keep on testing it, although in my case anyways posting tweets is something I'd do manually as it's reporting."
- **Then:** "Bro I hope you're not mad at me or anything." (Nuance: the apology is the family dynamic showing; he experiences honest product feedback as a personal risk, which is exactly why his negatives are trustworthy and his continued participation is not market signal.)
- **His bar for ever posting a draft, pressed further:** "It isn't 'yet' hitting THAT benchmark. In simple words, if I see a generated tweet on Oparax, I ask myself 'is this how I'd write it, or at least 80% close to what I want'. Some pointers: Not all reports will have 'JUST IN' or 'BREAKING'. If there's a news which isn't huge, I simply put a '❗️' at the beginning. In addition, I usually cite the @ of the journalist who posted that article rather than the media itself. These small details are what I'm looking into."
- **His balanced close:** "I personally wouldn't say it overreacts, because it's a work in progress. For the time so far, and compared to the initial model, this is really good. I'll still say that if these small details are understood by the AI, in addition to the speed, it can be a real game changer."
- **Learning:** the used half of the product is monitoring; drafting competes with his craft. His posting bar is concrete and unmet: 80 percent voice resemblance, correct label usage (not every post gets "JUST IN"/"BREAKING", minor news opens with a bare ❗️), and citing the journalist's handle rather than the outlet.

## Epoch 6: August 12 to 25, the retention test and the seven answers

- **Ten days idle** the moment daily personal contact from the founder stopped, with roughly 429 drafts accumulating unread. His four-day usage burst (Aug 8 to 12) coincided exactly with daily contact and stopped when it stopped. This is the strongest falsification in the record: even the best-case user, family, does not return unprompted.
- **Seven structured questions** were sent 2026-08-22 (what he missed during the gap, why he opened it when he did, whether Oparax ever showed him something first, what he did after the Deco error, the bar for autonomous posting, what "recommended" actually meant, and a direct price question). He answered on 2026-08-25, verbatim below.

**Q1, did he miss anything while not opening Oparax:** "I didn't miss out on anything as such, no, - other than the 1-2 minute delay, it was all very smooth and I was able to catch up on anything. In addition, Oparax allowed me to make sure I covered everything in a particular news or report."

**Q2, what made him open it:** "It was mostly me catching up and seeing if I hadn't missed out on anything. If i did, I'd use Oparax's tweet as a reference."

**Q3, did it ever show him something first:** "To be very honest, no. I've not come across anything like this before."

**Q4, what he did after the Deco error:** "I had skipped it."

**Q5, what would have to be true before autonomous posting:** "To begin with, the basic, it needs to ensure that it gets the translation correct - because that's what it's all about. Minor details, nuances, paraphrasing, are all aspects which AI needs to cover. Rather than going through the article and simply translating what it says word by word, the AI should understand the details mentioned above and then give an output accordingly - as though it first tried understanding what the piece is about before giving the results."

**Q6, what "recommended" actually meant:** "Under 'recommendation', I had meant that as a small area in the 'sources' section. This would basically make things easier for someone. How I imagined it to be was: AI automatically scans and suggests the sources for you based on the 3-4 sources you've entered already."

**Q7, would he pay, and where is the no:** "Since I want to be honest, at this stage, $4 USD per month"

**What the answers establish:**

- **The speed advantage never materialized:** not once did Oparax show him news before he had seen it elsewhere (Q3), and during ten days away he missed nothing he learned late (Q1). For him the pain is not being first, it is completeness: "Oparax allowed me to make sure I covered everything in a particular news or report." The product he values is a coverage checker, not a speed edge.
- **Drafts have value as reference, not as posts:** when he had missed something, "I'd use Oparax's tweet as a reference" (Q2). The drafting layer's real use for him is summary material he rewrites, consistent with the Epoch 5 verdict.
- **The accuracy failure did not destroy trust:** he skipped the bad item and kept using the feed (Q4). But his autonomy bar (Q5) is understanding-first translation: comprehension of nuance and paraphrase before output, not word-by-word rendering.
- **"Recommended" was a source-discovery ask, not a feed ask (Q6):** he wants the system to suggest new sources from the ones already entered. This corrects the earlier reading of his Epoch 3 request.
- **The first price point in the record (Q7):** $4 a month, roughly 1 percent of his X income. Per the questioning rules this is a stated number, not a commitment; no money has moved. But as an anchor it says the perceived value today sits at pocket-change level even for the most favorable user in the record.

## Validated vs unvalidated

- **Validated (he raised it or did it):** monitoring and catch-up value, automatic source suggestions (his clarified "recommended" ask), speed as a stated concern (raised three separate times, though Q3 shows the speed edge never actually materialized for him), a visible source link (existed but was undiscoverable), the accuracy bar, the broad-desk behavior, drafts as reference material.
- **Stated but not committed:** $4 a month willingness to pay. A number in a message, not money moved.
- **Unvalidated (founder proposed, he merely agreed):** Slack alerts, four to five draft variants, correction capture, clustering. The founder flagged this pattern himself as probable confirmation bias. Treat all four as untested until someone asks for them or uses them.
