# The plan: the whole product in one feature (DRAFT)

## What happens

1. Landing. A visitor lands on oparax.ai. The page explains what Oparax does, how the alerts work and what it costs, shows the owner's own agent as a real example, and carries the box: an X handle and one sentence, the beat.
2. Building. On submit, oparax.ai/<handle> opens in its building state and shows the three steps as they happen: looking the person up on X, reading their newest posts, choosing sources and X accounts. Onboarding ends by writing a detailed brief on the person, saved once. Progress is saved as it goes, so closing the tab never starts a second build; reopening the page shows where it is. Typing a handle that already has a page opens that page.
3. The agent page. The person's picture and name, the beat, the brief, up to ten sites and feeds with one sentence each on why they fit, at least five X accounts, and the feed. The feed starts at once with each source's ten newest items from the last two days. New articles keep arriving on their own.
4. The feed. Every new article is judged against the beat with the brief in view. Articles that fit become cards: a headline and one to five facts, each with its quote from the article, checked against the article. Reports of one story fold into one story card listing its sources. Two views, single articles and stories, and a "skipped" list of what was judged off the beat, with its score. Posts from the watched X accounts go through the same judging, grouping and cards, with the account's handle and name on the card.
5. The story link. Each story has its own address; opening it shows the page with that card in view: headline, facts with their sources, the publishers, the time, an image when a source had one. This is what the bot links to.
6. The bot. A button opens the person's own X composer addressed to @oparax_ai with a one-time code. Sending it from the X account the page belongs to connects the two; a message from any other account is ignored. From then on the bot sends one DM per story, ever: headline, first fact, the story link. Hobby and Creator get one DM a day at 2 pm their local time; Wire gets a mini digest every 15 minutes when there is news. STOP, PAUSE and RESUME as replies do what they say.
7. The free week. The first view in a real browser by anyone but the owner starts the clock; the owner's own visits, automatic link previews and bots never do. The page then shows the price and the days left. During the week the recommended X accounts are watched, up to 300 posts. The page is read-only for everyone until it is paid for. A page nobody views pauses after 14 days and restarts on its first view.
8. Day seven. The page freezes: old cards stay readable, nothing new arrives, and the page shows the three prices: Hobby $5, Creator $30, Wire $99 a month. Paying through Stripe checkout is the sign-up: the email typed at checkout creates or finds the account, binds the page to it, and unfreezes it. The person gets a sign-in link by email. No card before day seven.
9. Sign-in. The login page offers email, Continue with Google and Continue with X. A signed-in person whose email has no agent sees the landing box and sets one up.
10. Settings, after paying. Add or remove sources; mark a source "do not filter"; pick which X accounts to watch, each showing its posts per day and the running total against the monthly pool, with one warning when an account alone would empty the pool in under ten days; set the DM hour; switch on the GitHub and Product Hunt digests; manage the subscription and cancel through Stripe's portal. When the pool hits zero the accounts pause until the month resets, sites continue, and the page says so.
11. Digests. With GitHub on, code turns the beat into a few topics, searches GitHub daily for new repos past 100 stars or rising fast, has Jev judge each against the beat, and shows the fits as cards: name, a why-now line, the repo's own description. The person can name repos they follow and a star threshold (3,000 by default); a card appears when one passes it. Product Hunt is the same shape with its daily launches.
12. Public pages. The privacy and terms text says what the product now does. The Contact dialog takes an email and a message, stores it and emails no-reply@oparax.ai.

## What happens when it fails

- The handle does not exist on X: "Handle not found" under the box. X itself unavailable: the page says the lookup could not finish and offers to try again.
- A build stops partway: the page shows which step failed and a "Try again" that resumes the saved work; only the extra work is charged and the page's reservation stays.
- The $200 is used up: the box says builds are unavailable for now and offers to leave a handle; nothing promises a reset.
- A source cannot be read: the page marks it "could not read its last N items"; a readable item clears the mark. No card is ever written from a teaser.
- A fact fails a check: the writer gets one repair pass; what still fails is withheld, never shown. With no fact left, the page shows the article's title as an unverified report with its link and sends no DM. A failed rewrite keeps the previous checked card.
- A judgment or write fails on the model's side: the item stays pending and is retried on the next run; the page tells "no relevant news yet" apart from "could not process".
- The bot cannot deliver: the story stays on the page, is marked undelivered for the owner, and is never sent twice. A send whose outcome is unknown is held for the owner, not retried.
- The pool is empty: the accounts pause, the page says so, sites continue.
- Renewal payment fails: the feed runs until the paid-through date, then freezes with "payment failed, update your card" and a link to the Stripe portal; a later successful payment unfreezes it.
- Payment confirmed but the account not yet connected: the return page says the account is being connected and to check the email; nothing paid is lost.
- A sign-in link has expired: the login page offers a new one.
- The first view's location is unknown: the DM hour defaults to 2 pm UTC until the person sets it.
- A source spends more than $1 in a day: it is paused, marked on the page and in the operational record; a busy but cheap source is never touched.
- X credits low (under $15 by our own count) or the daily balance read fails: new anonymous builds and free-week X polling pause; paying users keep running; the owner sees the last known balance.
