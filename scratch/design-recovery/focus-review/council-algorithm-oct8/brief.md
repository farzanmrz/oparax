## THE DRAFT (verify this)

Two inputs: the X handle and the sentence, which the code calls the beat. Code does every X call. Two models judge: Jev, a cheap yes-or-no judge, and GPT-6 Luna Fast, which picks and writes once. The left column of every run page lists these 7 steps, and the log line under each step is the exact text the engine writes.

1. Look up the person. Code asks X for the handle: name, bio, picture, website and pinned post. A missing handle is a form error, nothing is spent. The page's right column appears here with the picture, name, bio as About, and the sentence as Brief. Log: "Found Example Builder on X". Page: run?at=profile.

2. Read their posts. Code reads their own posts from the last 90 days until it has the newest 10. A thread counts as 1 post; reposts and replies to others are left out. At most 10 pages of X's timeline are read. Log: "Read 10 newest posts". The posts appear on the right. Page: run?at=posts.

3. Gather candidates. Code builds the candidate list: every row of the shared table of 150 sources, plus every account the person quoted in those posts, unless the quote was in a sponsored post. A mention does not count (owner, September 27). Log: "Gathered 150 from the source list, 1 accounts you quoted". Page: run?at=scoring (the "Gathering candidates" block).

4. Jev scores every candidate. Jev receives the beat, the bio, the 10 posts, the candidates, and 1 question per candidate. After the October 8 fix, that is 3 requests instead of 1, each carrying only its own candidates. The question (verbatim from engine.ts ROW_Q): "Is `rows.<id>` a useful recurring source for monitoring `beat`, based on the supplied description or the account's bio and authored posts? Use `person.bio`, `posts` and `pinned_post` only to clarify interests within the beat. The beat alone can justify a match; absence from the sampled activity is not evidence against it. Being quoted, mentioned or pinned does not by itself establish suitability. Judge what the source publishes, not the fact that the person interacted with it." Jev answers each with a probability. Anything at 0.35 or above passes. The page shows the passed ones as "Strong match" from 0.75 up and "Possible match" from 0.35 up, never the number. Log: "Jev kept 41 of 151 candidates". Measured September 27 for Liam: 107 of 150 passed, under a cent, about 14 seconds. Pages: run?at=scoring (an amber activity line while scoring), run?at=jev (the bands).

THE BUG (fixed October 8, commit ddcaf857): the scoring call sent all 156 candidates in every request. jev.ts splits the questions into batches of 60 but each batch's request carried the whole state; the guard estimates tokens as JSON length / 2 and refuses above 32,000; the table alone estimates about 41,000, so every real build died here with `state_too_large` since the product build of September 28 (the September 27 lab run passed because the cap did not exist yet). The page showed "Preparation could not finish" and the generic failed state (page: /local-preview/failed shows the failed look with a different cause). The fix: scoreRows splits the candidates by serialized size (about 24,000 estimated tokens per request including the shared context) and merges the score maps; same question, same threshold, same single checkpoint.

5. The model picks and writes. GPT-6 Luna Fast gets 1 message: the beat, the profile with bio and pinned post, the 10 posts with their pictures attached, and only the candidates that passed, highest score first, with their scores. Its instructions (prompts.ts) say: sites up to 10 rows of kind rss or website, prefer a source the person links or quotes, fewer is fine, a feed over a news page when equal, skip a stream that repeats another pick; accounts at least 5, only from the candidate rows and, after the search, the authors it returned; one "why" sentence per pick. It returns 1 structured answer: sites, accounts, a reason each, and the brief: a summary under 1,200 characters, interests, languages, 3 to 8 topic terms for the daily digests, and a search field (terms or null). Reasoning effort high, up to 6,000 output tokens, a 120-second timeout, no retries. Log: "Choosing recommendations and writing the brief". Page: run?at=chosen (pills under X accounts, feeds and websites; the brief summary in the right column).

6. One X search, only if needed. If fewer than 5 accounts fit and the model named terms (and this is the first turn), code runs 1 X search: full archive endpoint, last 30 days, 10 results, no replies or reposts. Jev scores the new authors with the same question; only those at 0.35 or above are shown to the model, which answers a second and final time with "search null". Never a second search. Log: "Searching X for accounts: <terms>", or "Enough accounts already fit" when the answer already has 5. No example page shows this step.

7. Check and save. Code trusts nothing by name: a site is kept only if it is a real table row that Jev passed, at most 10; an account is kept only if Jev passed it (as a candidate or a search author), because models invent handles with full confidence (decisions.md). Then the monitor is saved (sources, accounts, brief, profile, posts), the free week starts, and the page says "Your agent is ready" with "Open your feed". Log: "Saving your agent". Page: run?at=done.
