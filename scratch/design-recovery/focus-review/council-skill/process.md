# What produced the accepted renders, versus what produced the rejected ones (host's account, not data)

Everything below is checkable in `focus-review/` (RUN-STATE.md progress log, the council and render folders).

## Inputs that existed for the accepted round and not before
1. The owner's own words, verbatim and in order (`council-design/owner-today.md`), not the host's summary of them.
2. A board of real screenshots (`board/`, 130 images): his 6 picks first, 109 viewport captures of 21 Linear and Supabase pages taken by agents in a headless browser, and 15 Oparax renders each paired with his verbatim verdict, praised and rejected (`board/OPARAX-VERDICTS.md`). His words: "a lot of this is just briefs."
3. His points from the evening turned into 10 acceptance criteria every direction had to meet (`council-design/brief.md`), after the host re-read his messages and listed what the previous render ignored: variation between directions, a visible accent like Supabase's green, several functional hues "in the relevant location", soft shadows and gentle gradients, a live product window lit against the background, complexity shown neatly (shell, tiles, charts), React Bits animations, imaginative directions, hue per source kind with a faint tint, dark on dark that still separates. The brief said that where research conflicts with these 10, the 10 win.
4. A 9-day pattern of his 737 complaints and 164 praise lines from Claude Code and Codex sessions, with a skeptic's corrections (`theme-research/owner-pattern.md`).

## Steps that differed
1. The council (Astra, Grok, Kimi) was asked to name the difference "as a human looking at" his screenshots next to ours, to cite board images by filename for every proposal, and to propose 3 directions that look clearly different from each other. They converged on one diagnosis (Kimi: "his pages show many small machines working; ours show paragraphs in boxes") and three compositions (Window, Newsroom, Deck), reconciled in `council-design/agreed.md`.
2. One Opus builder designed against the images, not the text: it opened the owner picks, praised and rejected renders and about 12 reference captures before building; used real data only (it fetched 6 more real reports from configured sources with verbatim spans, real favicons and avatars at runtime); used the React Bits Pro and AI Elements patterns as composition, recolored to one palette; and screenshotted its own work beside the references and the rejected renders, iterating until it could not be mistaken for them.
3. The owner saw rendered pages in his browser, dark and light, with plain explanations, not a plan.

## What went wrong before (for contrast)
- Text briefs scoped to "structure only, ignore polish", so builders assembled stock parts and the council audited truth, not whether it lands.
- Research measurements turned into rules ("color only as 6 to 14px dots, 0.5 percent budget"), which made the page less alive.
- Palettes compared on one unchanged composition (four shades of one page).
- The host summarized the owner instead of quoting him, and lost points he had already made.
- Some of the cost was the owner's own locked constraints (for example "nothing else" on the card), which the council surfaced as yes or no questions rather than silent changes.
