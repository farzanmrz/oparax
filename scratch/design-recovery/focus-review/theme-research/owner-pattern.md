## 3. The Pattern

### 3.1 In Plain Words He judges a page the way a first-time visitor sees it, in one glance, and three things fail that glance over and over. First, space: either the content sits in a narrow column with dead bands on both sides, or elements are blown up, so the page feels empty. Second, signal: everything is the same dark surface with one blue smeared across it, so nothing stands apart, nothing looks alive, and columns do not line up; what he admires in Linear and Supabase is the opposite, near-black neutrals that are still layered by faint steps of light, borders and soft shadow, with color appearing only where it means something (a status, a source logo, the one primary action). Third, truth: the page should show the real product doing its real job (real tweet in, Oparax deciding, real X DM out) exactly once, without fictional examples, captions, labels or repeated sections; and anything he already settled should stay settled. When those three hold, he praises the result even when it is plain ("Everything feels a bit plain, which is acceptable"); when they fail, no amount of new components helps.

### 3.2 Rules a Designer Can Apply
1. **Never Cap Width With Fixed Pixels:** Side space is a modest percentage and content fills its container; no element is larger than its information needs. Tied to: "how the fuck is it still having those massive margins" (09-28 21:49), "responsive percentage-wise measurements" (09-28 23:46), "now the images are huge for no damn reason I was looking for a midway point" (09-17 18:23). Do not go to zero either: "Margins are too thin now" (09-30 10:21).
2. **Never Tint Surfaces With the Accent:** Backgrounds, cards and body text stay black and grey; blue marks only the primary action, focus and active state. Extra hues appear only as functional signals. Tied to: "too blue and too just monotone" (10-02 01:51), "The color is coming from functional stuff" (10-02 02:12), "Color for functional stuff is fine" (10-02 02:16).
3. **Always Separate by Light, Not Hue:** Each surface layer is a small luminance step above the one below, with a visible hairline border and a soft shadow or faint glow; check light mode explicitly. Tied to: "dark card on a dark card ... It looks good" (09-29 04:05), "borders are too light ... in light mode, it looks very bland" (10-01 22:49), "not aggressive gradients" (10-02 02:16).
4. **Always Show the Real Thing:** Real tweet anatomy, real X DM, official colored logos, actual product UI in motion; never write "illustrative" or "real example". Tied to: "I literally wanted you to show how a tweet looks" (09-29 08:38), "Your logos are so flat" (09-30 10:21), "the real product window, which appears very cleanly as a card" (09-30 22:15).
5. **Never Say Anything Twice:** One idea per section; sources, dates, logo and sign-up each appear once; no label for what color or layout already shows. Tied to: "each section should be unique and have one idea" (09-29 08:41), "three sources at the top, and then you're showing them at the bottom also" (09-30 23:56), "Every planned block doesn't need to say \"planned.\"" (10-02 00:39).
6. **Always Align Columns From the Top:** Parallel columns share header line, top edge and footer treatment; centered text is centered on the page. Tied to: "not aligned on the same line" and "aligned from the top in a clean, visual manner" (09-30 23:56), "misaligned" (10-02 01:35).
7. **Never Reopen a Settled Choice or Copy a Reference:** Dark default, white logo mark, chosen sans font, no pause button, no custom components hold until he changes them; a liked component is inspiration. Tied to: "did we not do away with that?" (09-30 09:38), "you start using it directly" (10-01 23:13), "imaginative yet fit within some existing bounds" (09-29 00:25).

On 10-02 02:12 he also softened: "me insisting that everything should be stretched out ... might be a bit overrated", while disliking Supabase's "extreme margins". **Later and stronger:** moderate, proportional side space; neither a narrow column nor edge to edge.

5. **Blue Accent vs Too Blue:** "Try and make it with a blue accent, right?" (10-01 22:49) versus "extremely blue" (10-02 00:38). **Both stand:** blue as an accent, never as the page tint.

## 5. Mapping to Linear and Supabase

This is what the two sites do that matches his complaints, from public observation of their marketing and app UI; check the detailed numbers in `study-linear.md` and `supabase.md` in this folder before quoting values.

1. **Separation Without Hue:** Linear builds its dark UI from a near-black base and a few surface steps only a few percent lighter, with low-alpha white hairline borders and a faint top-edge highlight on cards. Supabase uses a stepped neutral gray scale (Radix-style) where background, surface, border and text each sit on a defined step. Both answer theme 1.3 directly: elements separate through luminance and borders, so a dark card on a dark page still reads. Our renders used one or two dark values and a blue-tinted border, so cards dissolved, and light mode borders were near invisible.
2. **Where Color Lives:** In Linear, color sits on issue status icons, priority markers, labels, avatars and integration logos; surfaces and body text stay neutral gray at several text levels (primary, secondary, muted). Supabase reserves its green for the primary button, links, active nav and focus, and shows partner logos in their own colors. That is his "functional color" and "life". Our renders put blue on backgrounds, headings, chips, glows and diagrams at once, which is why he sees "at max 2 colours" and "too blue" at the same time.
3. **Gradients and Glow:** Linear uses soft radial glows and gentle gradients mostly in hero regions and around product imagery, at low opacity, plus slight inner highlights on panels. They read as light falling on surfaces, not decoration. His word "polished" points here. Our renders used either flat fills or saturated blue panels and backgrounds (the "Oparax's blue background thing in the diagram" he rejected 09-29 07:44).
4. **The Hero Is the Product:** Linear's landing shows real app UI (issue lists, intake, integrations) framed as a large card on the dark page; Supabase shows real dashboard, table editor and code. That is rule 4 and theme 1.7. Our heroes showed abstract flows, an Oparax logo node and fictional space stories.
5. **Typography Hierarchy by Gray, Not Weight or Color:** Both sites carry hierarchy mostly through size and text-gray steps with restrained weights. This matches his bold rules (normal display, bold labels) and his "too much bolding" note.
6. **Where They Do Not Match Him:** Supabase's landing sections have wide side margins he called "extreme margins, which I don't like"; Linear's marketing content also sits in a constrained width with generous vertical spacing. Copy their surface, border, text and color-placement system, not their page widths. Neither site is a feed reader, so the card anatomy must come from his card ruling (headline, fact lines with sources, sources shown once), not from their layouts.

## 6. What Past Renders Did That Triggered Him Most

Ranked by how often the same render behavior drew a complaint:

1. **Width caps and gutters in the design contract:** a fixed pixel content cap and narrow column re-created the margin problem on every new page, after he was told it was fixed (09-28 22:02 "you told me that the margins issue has been sorted, but apparently it hasn't").
2. **Repeated content and extra labels:** sources at top and bottom of a card, logo in several places, duplicate sign-up buttons, a timeline restating the hero, "planned", "Available Today", "Matches your interest" lines, long footers with logo and wordmark.
3. **Accent spread everywhere:** blue backgrounds and panels, blue-tinted surfaces, or the opposite swing to clashing hues and React Bits default pinks and purples (10-01 22:49 "so much pink and purple and shit emerging").
4. **Settled choices reverting:** Hanken Grotesk and JetBrains Mono returning, light mode as default, a pause/play button, the logo recolored or swapped to a dark version in light mode, bold titles, custom components after "Switch to Mira".
5. **Fictional or approximate content:** NASA, Europa Clipper, space telescopes, "Harbor Cities FC" topics, tweet mocks that do not look like tweets, flat monochrome logos, "illustrative" and "real example" captions.
6. **Inspiration copied literally:** Magic Transform and Circle Center Flow built as-is after he named them as inspiration; a big agent circle in the roadmap middle.
7. **Misaligned parallel columns:** Sources, Oparax and Delivery headers on different lines, gray footers in two of three columns, a stray right-aligned intro line.
8. **Light mode untested:** borders, separators and buttons nearly invisible; logo changing color.
9. **Process misses that produced the above:** wireframes instead of rendered components, renders built before the council reviewed them, and literal edits to his words rather than solving what he meant (09-26 01:32 "the page was just changed literally as per what I said, and not logically to what I will need").
