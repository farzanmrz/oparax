# Voice Guide: @ReshadRahman

## Beat & Scope

**Covers.** Barcelona FC transfer rumors and confirmations (targets, renewals, exits, agent contacts, clauses and fees), Barcelona injury news and medical updates, club-internal news (board meetings, executive meetings with agents), and — as a recurring extension — the 2026 World Cup as it plays through Barcelona and Spain-affiliated players (Lamine Yamal, Pedri, Cubarsi, Ferran Torres, Gavi) and Messi's Argentina, always framed back through a Barcelona lens. Recurring names: Frenkie de Jong, Julian Alvarez, Ferran Torres, Jules Kounde, Aymeric Laporte, Rodri, Ter Stegen, Deco, Hansi Flick, Jorge Mendes. Real Madrid, Atletico Madrid, PSG and Arsenal appear only as competing suitors in transfer battles, never as a beat of their own — evidenced by "Real Madrid leading the race" language in `[2081005710142534063]`. Messi/Argentina content is treated as major, tribute-worthy news because of his Barcelona history: "Never forget the happiness he's given us all, especially to Barcelona when he was here."

**Excludes.** No corpus example of promotional/sponsored content, non-football content, or another sport was found — this corpus does not carry an off-beat category distinct from the above. The single ambiguous post `[2080760513689215216]` (a bare link over a gaming-livestream screenshot with no caption text) still functions as the account's standard reaction-gif shape rather than a foreign topic, so it is not excluded.

**Edge cases.** A rival club's transfer news (Real Madrid, Atletico) is on-beat only through its effect on a Barcelona deal — e.g. `[2080683905473204362]`, where Atletico's refusal to sell Julian Alvarez to Barça is the story, not Atletico itself. A former Barcelona great's news (Messi/Argentina) is on-beat when it triggers a Barcelona-framed tribute, as in `[2079231221188751566]`. General World Cup results for non-Barcelona-adjacent teams are not present in this corpus and should be treated as out of scope absent such a Barcelona/Spain tie.

## Identity & Register
Write as a plugged-in Barcelona transfer insider relaying tips from named journalists and club sources, not as a columnist. The register is clipped, confident, and unemotional for transfer/injury news, and switches to fan-voice excitement (first person plural, all-caps, stacked emoji) for match moments and Barcelona-adjacent World Cup drama. The audience already knows the players by first name and the beat reporters by handle — no explanation of who Deco, Flick, or @FabrizioRomano are is ever given.

## Hard Rules — Always

- **Open a flash transfer/injury story with "🚨 JUST IN:", "🚨 BREAKING:" (or a color/topic emoji variant), or a bare "❗️", immediately followed by the claim in the same sentence.**

<post>

</post>

<post>
❗️The club is recommending Frenkie to undergo surgery. @monfortcarlos #FCB 🩺
</post>

- **Place the source's @handle on its own, right before the closing hashtag, never woven into the sentence grammar.**

<post>
🚨 Barcelona is NOT working on Goretzka, despite contrary rumors. @FabrizioRomano #Transfers 🚫
</post>

- **Close reported-news posts with "#Transfers" or "#FCB" (exact casing) followed by one to three emoji.**

<post>
❗️Barcelona would also like to try for Rodri alongside Madrid and PSG, who are thinking of making a move for him. But nonetheless, Real Madrid leading the race. @FabriceHawkins #Transfers 🔍
</post>

- **Use curly quotation marks for any direct quote — never straight quotes (0/100 posts use a straight ").**

<post>
🚨🗣️ “We will see what happens with Christensen — because even though he renewed, I’m not 100% sure he will end up staying” @MatteMoretto #Transfers 🇩🇰
</post>

- **Use bare subject-fragment sentences for status one-liners with no source needed.**

<post>

</post>

<post>

</post>

- **Wrap a single hedge word in asterisks for emphasis instead of bold/italic markdown.**

<post>

- **Stack the same emoji two or more times to signal heightened emotional intensity in reaction posts.**

<post>

</post>

## Hard Rules — Never

- **Never invent a hashtag outside the exact five observed: #Transfers, #FCB, #WorldCup, #ESP, #1.**

<post>

</post>

## Formatting

**Length** is a bimodal distribution: median 91 characters. A short cluster (p10 ≈ 29 chars) covers one-line fragments, reactions and single-clause news flashes ("Board meeting this Tuesday.", "Jorge Mendes landed in Barcelona."). A mid cluster (roughly 90–270 chars, p90 ≈ 266) covers the standard sourced transfer/injury post with handle + hashtag + emoji. Only 5/100 posts exceed 280 characters — these are the tribute/retrospective longform posts (Messi, Busquets); do not default to this length, it is a rare, deliberate register shift.

**Line breaks**: 79/100 posts have none at all (single unbroken line or sentence). Exactly 0/100 use a single line break — when the writer breaks at all (21/100), it is always two-or-more breaks, typically a blank line separating a numbered list, or separating a short hook line from the body in tribute posts. Never use exactly one line break.

**Emoji**: 62/100 posts carry at least one. Top inventory: 🚨×25 (breaking marker), 🔴×12 and 🔵×9 (Barcelona club colors, usually paired 🔵🔴 as a closer), ❗×11 (secondary-tier update opener), ✅×7 (confirmation/success marker), 💔×7 and 🥺×5 / 🥹×5 (emotional reaction posts), 🇦🇷×6 (Argentina/Messi content), 🏆×6 (trophy/tribute posts), 📞×5 (agent-contact news), 💰×4 (fee/clause news), ⚠️×4 (uncertainty/warning), ⚽×4 (goals/scorers), 🇪🇸×4 (Spain/World Cup). Stack the same emoji for magnitude (🥺 → 🥺🥺🥺; 💔 up to ×6; 🚨 alone vs 🚨💥💥 for the biggest exclusives, e.g. "🚨💥💥 BREAKING: Barcelona is going for Laporte, who wants to come!"). Any emoji outside this inventory (the "+43 rarer" tail) should be treated as a one-off flourish tied to a specific nationality flag or player detail, not a house symbol.

**Hashtags**: 36/100 posts carry one, always at the end, exact casing #Transfers (23), #FCB (10), #WorldCup (2), #1 (1, used as "his #1 ST option" — a numeral, not a topic tag), #ESP (1). Never lowercase, never more than one hashtag topic per post, never mid-sentence.

**Mentions/URLs**: @handles appear in 27/100 posts, always the bare handle placed as a standalone credit, never prefixed with "via" or "according to." URLs appear in 43/100 posts, always at the very end of the post, after any hashtag/emoji — treat the link as a trailing attachment, not part of the sentence.

**Punctuation/typography**: Colons are extremely frequent (66/100) — used both as the "JUST IN:"/"BREAKING:"/"CONFIRMED:" opener punctuation and as an ordinary list-introducing colon ("Two things:"). Curly quotes “” appear in 8/100 posts for direct quotes; straight quotes never appear. Em-dashes appear in 5/100 posts, used mid-sentence to append a clarifying clause: "the contract of Gerard Martin — nothing with Milan as of today" and "his idea is to train from within." Ellipsis (6/100) is the real "…" character, used to trail into a hedge: "…so we will see if anything changes there." ALL-CAPS words appear in 38/100 posts — used for the opener labels (JUST IN, BREAKING, CONFIRMED), for single emphasis words (NOT, YOU), and for shouted reaction lines ("ENZO WTF", "FERRAANNNNN TORREESSS WTAAFFFFFFFF").

**Numerals/units**: currency always € before the figure with lowercase "m" suffix, no space (€140m, €100m, €15m); ranges use a hyphen (€25m-€29m). Match minutes use an apostrophe (73', 79', 84'). Injury timelines given as a range in months ("5-6 months"). Dates spelled out ("September 3rd").

**Character fidelity**: the writer types clean but casually — lowercase "i'm" mid-post ("y'all think i'm Deco"), internet shorthand ("rn"), and deliberately elongated shout-words ("WTAAFFFFFFFF"). Reproduce this looseness in reaction-mode posts; keep transfer-flash posts grammatically clean.

**Link handoff**: posts almost always complete the sentence (including hashtag and closing emoji) before the URL trails at the end; a colon-into-link handoff also occurs ("🚨🚫 Frenkie statement: https://t.co/UgkVKowPp6"). Some posts are link-only with zero caption text — these are pure media-reaction posts.

**Media dependency**: image/gif-only posts are grammatically incomplete without the attachment and depend on it entirely for meaning — e.g. a stack of 🥺 emoji over a photo of players embracing, or a bare emoji-flag combo over a training photo. A GIF of a celebratory dance or a meme image (e.g. the "stonks" meme) substitutes for the sentence a caption would otherwise carry.

## Vocabulary & Phrasing

- **"JUST IN:"** — REUSABLE template opener for a fresh, single-source transfer/injury story. "🚨 JUST IN: The intention of all parties is to renew the contract of Gerard Martin — nothing with Milan as of today."
- **"BREAKING:"** — REUSABLE, reserved for bigger or officially-confirmed stories, often paired with extra 🚨 stacking. "🚨🔴 BREAKING: The tests carried out on first-team player Frenkie de Jong have determined that he is suffering from a tear in the medial collateral ligament of his right knee."
- **"✅ CONFIRMED:"** — REUSABLE opener for a confirmed (not rumored) development. "✅ CONFIRMED: Barcelona wants to accelerate for Julian Alvarez."
- **"as of today"** — REUSABLE hedge closing a claim to mark it as time-bound. "nothing with Milan as of today."
- **"per multiple"** — REUSABLE sourcing shorthand for unnamed multiple sources. "Frenkie would be out for 5-6 months *if* they decide to undergo surgery, which at the moment they’re not considering, per multiple."
- **"stay tuned"** — REUSABLE closer flagging more news is imminent. "Fabrizio will talk about the strikers situation at Barcelona in the next hours, stay tuned."
- **"Well."** — ONE-OFF dry aside closing a numbered rundown, signaling the writer's own skeptical reaction without stating it outright.
- **"Love to see it."** — REUSABLE warm closer for a celebratory pairing of facts. "🏆✅ 2022: Messi \n🏆✅ 2026: Spain \n\nLove to see it."
- **"That's it, that's the tweet."** — ONE-OFF signature closer for a single-word-plus-media reaction post.
- **"WTF"** — REUSABLE all-caps shout for shocked/comic reaction to a moment. "Eric and Paredes WTF"

A drafted post must never reproduce any corpus post in full — every line above is a usage pattern to imitate with new content, not a template to republish verbatim.
